# Magnifica Humanitas → Responsible AI

Materials for a 30-minute session (15 min presented, 15 min discussion) reading Leo XIV's
encyclical *Magnifica Humanitas* (15 May 2026) as external input to the Microsoft Responsible AI
Standard and ORA practice. The religious framing is acknowledged once and then set aside; the
document is treated as normative stakeholder input, the way a NIST framework or a civil-society
critique would be.

| File | What it is |
|------|------------|
| [magnifica-humanitas-exec-deck.pptx](./magnifica-humanitas-exec-deck.pptx) | 16-slide executive deck, 16:9, with speaker notes on every slide |
| [speaker-script.md](./speaker-script.md) | Word-for-word script, timing map, prepared Q&A, discussion starters, quote sheet |
| [build_deck.py](./build_deck.py) | Regenerates the deck (`python3 build_deck.py`, requires `python-pptx`) |

## Argument in one line

An external framework, written with no visibility into our process, converges on most of our
principles — and the three places it doesn't (recourse for affected people, the labour and
environmental conditions of production, worker agency as a measured outcome) are real gaps.

## The ask

1. Add a design-intent prompt to the Impact Assessment (§104)
2. Make recourse a first-class requirement, not a byproduct of operator oversight (§103, §105)
3. Publish trade-offs, not just outcomes, in Transparency Notes (§107)
4. Track one agency metric per assistive deployment (§150, §156)

Source: <https://www.vatican.va/content/leo-xiv/en/encyclicals/documents/20260515-magnifica-humanitas.html>
